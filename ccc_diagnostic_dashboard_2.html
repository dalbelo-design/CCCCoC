Policy implications from this week's signals
What the strategic plan should address
1. Revise shelter rules
Shelter rules are the #1 documented barrier. Pets, couples, curfews, and sobriety requirements are excluding people with nowhere else to go. Each rule needs a cost-benefit review against occupancy data.
2. ID recovery at point of sweep
3 people this week blocked by sweep-related ID loss. ID recovery must be part of every sweep protocol — not an afterthought. Without ID, shelter access is impossible by design.
3. Fund outreach-first entry
8 of 9 first-time contacts accepted a service or referral. Outreach is the most effective system entry point for people who have never engaged. The ROI is documented here — weekly.

About this tool

Built for the strategic plan

A real-time service system diagnostic for the Contra Costa County Homelessness Strategic Plan — implementation phase. Captures what HMIS and the PIT count can't see.

The 7 questions outreach workers answer
1. Where are you right now? (East / West / Central / Unincorporated)
2. Where did this person sleep last night?
3. Swept or displaced in the past 6 months?
4. Prior contact with shelter or navigation system?
5. If prior contact — what got in the way? (conditional)
6. What was provided at today's interaction?
7. Anything else to flag? (optional)
→ Open outreach form
Deployment

Tuesday and Thursday outreach runs. Workers fill out one form per person during or right after each service interaction. No app to install — works in any mobile browser in approximately 60–90 seconds.

What this produces for the coordinating committee
→
Weekly signal brief — who was reached, what barriers came up, what the system did or didn't do
→
Monthly barrier report — ranked list of shelter rules and system failures blocking access
→
Quarterly diagnostic — system performance score for strategic plan review

"People moving through the homelessness system are the best real-time test of how that system performs. This tool captures what HMIS records and the Point-in-Time count can't see — the experience behind the data."
