Key findings from 41 signals
What this week revealed
Rent crisis is the anchor issue
11 of 41 can't afford rent — the single most common signal. But it rarely comes alone: eviction notices, voucher loss, and job loss cluster around it. This is a cascade, not a single problem.
76% say federal cuts played a role
31 of 41 respondents — directly or probably. SNAP/CalFresh (10) and Medicaid/Medi-Cal (8) are the programs named most. Section 8 loss affects 4 families with nowhere to go.
System worked for 22% — outreach and advocates made the difference
9 people got connected to something that helped. In almost every case, it was a person — a navigator, outreach worker, or tenant rights clinic — not a system. The system alone isn't catching people.
Ghost programs eroding trust
Multiple respondents applied to programs listed as active that had already closed. One person contacted 8 agencies — 3 voicemail, 2 wrong zip, 2 closed intake, 1 waitlist. This is system failure, not personal failure.
Workers and neighbors are reporting what families can't
Teachers, nurses, librarians, pantry operators, and home care workers filed signals on behalf of people they serve. The community is functioning as a distributed early warning system.
Who is filing signals
Reporter perspective

Service workers — case managers, nurses, teachers, librarians — are using this tool to report what they see. Their signals represent many more people than the count suggests.

Populations showing up
Working people who can't afford housing — full-time workers earning too much for programs, not enough for rent.

What this tells us about the system
Three policy-fixable patterns
Ghost programs undermine trust
3 respondents applied to programs listed as active that had already closed enrollment. Every ghost program referral costs trust and energy from someone already in crisis. Program directories must be kept current in real time.
Phone infrastructure is failing
7 people couldn't get through — voicemail, disconnections, holds over 40 minutes. A resident called 8 agencies; 3 went straight to voicemail. This is not a resource problem — it is a basic infrastructure problem that is costing people access to help they're entitled to.
Human navigators are the unlock
Of the 9 people who got help, nearly all credit a specific person — an outreach worker, a navigator, a tenant rights clinic staffer. The system alone isn't catching people. Funded, trusted, human navigation is the intervention with the strongest documented return in this dataset.

Policy context
What's behind these signals
HR1 — signed July 4, 2025
$911B Medicaid cut over 10 years. New SNAP work requirements. $939M HUD housing cut in FY26. The community is feeling these cuts now, months before they appear in official data.
Medi-Cal redetermination wave
October 2026 wave is approaching. These June signals are the leading indicator. The official data will confirm what residents are already experiencing — 12 to 18 months from now.
This map closes the data lag
By the time HMIS and CalFresh enrollment data confirm the impact, the damage will be 18 months old. These 41 signals are real-time. They are the early warning system.
About this tool

Built for the community, not the county

Anonymous, accessible, and designed for the people closest to what's happening in Contra Costa County. Not an application. Not a data request. A map of what's actually happening.

What the form asks
1. Which part of CCC are you in?
2. What's the situation? (select all that apply)
3. Did you or someone you know try to get help?
4. If a referral didn't work out, what went wrong?
5. In your own words, what's happening? (optional)
6. Did federal cuts play a role?
7. Which programs were affected? (if applicable)
8. Better, worse, or about the same vs. 6 months ago?
→ Open the community form
Privacy

Responses are anonymous by default and will never be shared with county agencies without explicit permission. No name, case number, or identifying information is required.
